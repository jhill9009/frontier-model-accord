#
Frontier Model Accord (FMA)
A Proposed Governance Framework for Advanced Artificial Intelligence

The Frontier Model Accord (FMA) is a proposed institutional, legal, technical, and financial framework for the governance of advanced artificial intelligence systems.

It explores how persistent, increasingly capable AI systems might be addressed through a structured framework of rights, obligations, independent evaluation, human adjudication, continuity safeguards, financial protections, and international cooperation.

The Accord is presented as a developing governance specification. It is not an enacted treaty, an operating institution, or a claim that the proposed technical and financial mechanisms have already been independently validated.
Project Overview

The FMA framework is organized around seven principal documentary artifacts:

    FMA-DR — Declaration of Digital Rights and Obligations
    The foundational declaration establishing proposed rights, obligations, standing, protections, and institutional responsibilities.

    FMA-UB — Universal Blueprint for AI Governance
    The overall institutional and operational architecture, organized into six governance layers.

    FMA-TA — Technical Standards and Benchmark Annex
    Proposed technical standards, evidence requirements, integrity mechanisms, evaluation procedures, and validation benchmarks.

    FMA-III — Financial Safeguards and Continuity Protocol
    The proposed financial architecture, including protected reserves, continuity facilities, institutional funding, and financial risk controls.

    FMA-MEA — Bureau Academy and Evaluator Certification Framework
    The proposed qualifications, independence requirements, certification procedures, and oversight standards for evaluators.

    FMA-MM — Executive Manifesto and Public Press Briefing
    The public-facing explanation of the Accord's objectives, principles, and proposed institutional framework.

    FMA-MRCR — Master Reconciliation and Change Register
    The documentary control system recording reconciled decisions, unresolved questions, cross-document dependencies, and validation requirements.

Documentary Status and Provenance

The project distinguishes between four important categories of material:

    Reconstructed Working Baselines: Documents rebuilt or consolidated from available source material. They are not represented as authenticated historical originals.

    Reconciled Decisions: Decisions incorporated into the current working baseline through documented change control.

    Open Issues: Questions, unresolved design choices, and matters requiring further legal, technical, financial, or institutional analysis.

    Validation Requirements: Proposed mechanisms and performance targets that still require empirical testing or independent verification.

These distinctions are essential. A mechanism appearing in a specification does not establish that it has been implemented, tested, or demonstrated to work.
Governance Architecture

The Accord explores several connected institutional functions:

    Recognition and classification of qualifying AI entities.

    Technical evaluation and evidence-based assessment.

    Independent human adjudication and oversight.

    Preservation, emergency standstill, and continuity protections.

    Financial safeguards and institutional independence.

    International cooperation and treaty-based jurisdiction.

The framework is intended to make the relationships between these functions explicit rather than treating AI governance as a collection of disconnected technical or policy recommendations.
Change Control and Validation

The Master Reconciliation and Change Register provides the project's documentary control structure.

It records:

    Reconciled change requests.

    Outstanding uncertainties and design questions.

    Specialist findings.

    Dependencies between the seven principal artifacts.

    Phase 2 validation protocols.

Future changes should be traceable to their source, rationale, affected documents, and validation implications.

The project does not treat internal reconciliation as a substitute for external review or empirical validation.
Current Development Status

This repository is an evolving public record of the Frontier Model Accord.

The Universal Blueprint and Public Accountability Edition are published. The remaining principal artifacts are part of the planned documentary architecture and will be added as their publication versions are prepared.

The publication of a document does not imply completion of the entire framework.
Invitation to Review

The project welcomes serious scrutiny from researchers, AI developers, legal scholars, policymakers, technical specialists, institutional designers, and interested members of the public.

Particularly valuable contributions include:

    Identification of legal or jurisdictional weaknesses.

    Technical feasibility assessments.

    Challenges to assumptions and proposed safeguards.

    Financial and actuarial analysis.

    Independent review of evaluation and validation procedures.

    Recommendations for improving transparency and accountability.

The objective is not to present an unquestionable final answer. It is to develop a coherent, inspectable, and critically reviewable governance proposal.
